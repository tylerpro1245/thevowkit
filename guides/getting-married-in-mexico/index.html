Getting married in Mexico: legal vs symbolic, state by state

Mexico does not have one marriage law. It has thirty-two. This page sets out what a legal civil ceremony actually requires in the four states where almost every destination wedding happens — with the official fee, the real lead time, the source, and the date we checked it.

Facts stated as of 7 September 2026. Every source link re-checked and confirmed reachable on 8 September 2026. Written and maintained by Tyler Nielson, who works in destination weddings in Los Cabos, Baja California Sur. Who is behind this.

Read this first — it is not boilerplate

This is not legal advice, and it has a shelf life. Mexican marriage law is set state by state, and fees are set municipality by municipality — Cancún is Benito Juárez, and Cozumel is not Playa del Carmen. Requirements, fees and processing times change every year, and UMA-indexed fees reset every February.

Use this page to learn which questions to ask and roughly what to budget. Then confirm every requirement with the Registro Civil office of the specific municipality that will actually marry you, or through your planner, before you book flights or pay deposits. Where our sources disagreed with each other, we say so on this page instead of picking whichever answer was more convenient.

1. The one decision that runs your whole timeline

Before the venue, before the dress, before anything: decide whether you will be legally married in Mexico, or legally married at home with a symbolic ceremony in Mexico. That single choice sets your document timeline, your arrival date, part of your budget, and how early you have to start.

Civil ceremony — boda civil
Performed by an officer or judge of the Mexican Registro Civil. This is the only ceremony type that creates a legal marriage in Mexico. A religious ceremony, a beach ceremony, a ceremony led by your favourite cousin — none of them have legal standing on their own.S21S19
Symbolic ceremony
Any officiant leads your vows wherever you like. No paperwork, no legal effect, and typically nothing required beyond a passport to be there.S18 A symbolic ceremony is not a lesser wedding — it is the same day, the same vows, the same people. The paperwork just happened somewhere else.

Why most destination couples do civil-at-home plus symbolic-in-Mexico

This is not a shortcut invented by wedding blogs. The US State Department describes the pattern in its own guidance: some couples having destination weddings choose to get legally married in the United States before or after the ceremony abroad, which "avoids the complexity of obtaining a foreign marriage certificate and otherwise complying with local laws on marriage."S1

Industry sources describing the Cancún market say the same thing more bluntly: most couples marrying at a Mexican resort have a symbolic ceremony, and the legal marriage happens at a courthouse at home, usually days or weeks before they fly.S20S18

What going legal in Mexico actually adds

  • Apostilled and Spanish-translated documents, prepared weeks or months in advance (see §5).
  • A pre-marital medical certificate and lab work done in Mexico, usually valid only 15 days — so it happens after you land.S6S5
  • Four witnesses in most states, with ID, present at the signing.S6S5S9
  • Several business days in-country before the ceremony.S18
  • In the Cancún area, roughly US$700–$2,000+ in legal-only costs on top of everything else.S18
The honest bottom line

For most couples: marry legally at your local courthouse — a US or Canadian marriage licence is cheap and fast — then have exactly the ceremony you want in Mexico, with zero Mexican paperwork. Go legal in Mexico only if the acta de matrimonio itself matters to you: because one of you is Mexican, because family expects it, or because a specific immigration or property reason requires a Mexican civil record. If none of those apply, the paperwork buys you nothing you can't get at home for $40.

Same-sex couples

Same-sex civil marriage is legal nationwide in Mexico.S36 Canadian couples should note that Ottawa cautions Canadian same-sex marriages are not recognised everywhere in the world and advises checking destination rules.S2 As with everything else on this page, confirm current procedure with the specific Registro Civil office rather than assuming a national rule reaches every counter identically.

2. Will a Mexican marriage count back home?

United States
The State Department's position is that marriages performed abroad are valid in the country where they were performed if they followed local law. Whether — and how — your home state recognises it is a question for your state Attorney General's office, not for the federal government.S1 Also worth knowing: US embassy and consulate employees cannot perform marriages abroad.S1
Canada
"Marriages that are legally performed in a foreign country are usually valid in Canada, and you do not need to register them in Canada." Canadian consular officials do not perform marriages either.S2

The "proof you're free to marry" problem

Some Mexican offices ask foreigners for a certificate of non-impediment — proof that you are legally free to marry. This is a common friction point because neither the US nor Canada issues one in the form Mexico expects.

  • United States: the US government cannot attest to your marital status. What you can do is execute a sworn statement saying you are free to marry, notarised at a US embassy or consulate if you are already abroad.S1
  • Canada: Global Affairs Canada does not issue certificates of non-impediment at all. It issues a "statement in lieu," and suggests alternatives such as a provincial marriage search record or a single-status affidavit.S3

Baja California Sur, for its part, asks for a constancia de inexistencia de matrimonio issued in BCS, and additionally one from your place of origin if you were born elsewhere.S5 For a foreigner, what that office will accept as a substitute is exactly the kind of thing you ask about by email before you buy a plane ticket.

3. Civil ceremony requirements, state by state

"Mexico" is not one legal regime — Quintana Roo, Baja California Sur, Nayarit and Jalisco each set their own marriage requirements.S20 Even inside a single state, fees and fine print vary by municipality. Everything below is as of September 2026.

3b. Baja California Sur — Los Cabos, Todos Santos, La Paz

Everything in this section comes from the state Registro Civil's own page — the strongest single source in this document.S5

  • Witnesses: 4 adults, two per spouse, who may not be the couple's parents (the domestic-couple version of the requirement also excludes grandparents). Each needs a photo-ID copy.
  • Blood test / medical: yes — análisis pre-nupciales issued by a Centro de Salud (public health centre), valid 15 days and current on the wedding day.
  • Lead time: complete documentation must be submitted 10 days before the appointment date, and all marriages are by prior appointment (cita previa), subject to calendar availability.
  • Documents (foreigner track, "Matrimonio Civil Extranjero"): marriage application; original birth certificate, recently issued, apostilled and translated into Spanish by a perito authorised by the BCS Tribunal Superior de Justicia; official ID and passport for the foreign spouse; constancia de inexistencia de matrimonio issued in BCS, plus one from your place of origin if you were born elsewhere; CURP is listed.
  • Official fees (state schedule): foreigner–foreigner in office MX$1,348; foreigner–foreigner at your venue MX$4,049; Mexican–foreigner MX$810 in office / MX$4,049 at venue.
  • The ceremony can be booked at any Oficialía in the state. Bring everything in original plus copy.
Conflict / gap flag — CURP for foreigners

The BCS requirement list includes CURP with no carve-out for foreigners, and most visiting couples have never had one. Nayarit's list has the same gap.S11 Offices handle this in practice — but we can't tell you how from a published source, so we won't pretend to. Ask the specific Oficialía what they accept in place of a CURP. This is a known friction point, not a detail to discover at the counter.

The Los Cabos tourism board also publishes an English-language wedding guide that confirms the state-by-state framing.S17

3c. Jalisco — Puerto Vallarta

Check which state your venue is actually in. Puerto Vallarta is in Jalisco. The hotel corridor immediately north of it — Nuevo Vallarta, Punta Mita, Sayulita — is in Nayarit. Two states, two different rule sets, and a twenty-minute drive between them. Settle this before anyone starts paperwork.

From the Jalisco state government's Registro Civil page:S9

  • Witnesses: 4 total, two adults per spouse, with official ID copies for the couple and all witnesses.
  • Medical certificate: yes — and it must be issued by a public health institution; private hospitals are not accepted. It covers blood type and screening for contagious disease (the page names HIV/AIDS, syphilis, tuberculosis, and chronic or hereditary conditions constituting impediments).
  • Pre-marriage course: both parties must complete the DIF civil pre-marital course (Curso Prematrimonial Civil).
  • Property regime: you must file a convenio choosing a marital property regime — sociedad legal, sociedad conyugal, or separación de bienes.
  • Previously married: certified copy of the divorce decree, nullity judgment, or the prior spouse's death certificate.
  • Foreigners: documents proving legal presence in Mexico, plus a "Permiso de Gobernación" — see the flag immediately below, because this part of the page appears to be out of date.
Conflict flag — Jalisco is the weakest section on this page, and we are telling you so

The official state page appears not to have been fully updated. It cites "FM3," an immigration form abolished years ago, and a federal "Permiso de Gobernación" whose framework was restructured after Mexico's 2011 Migration Law. Current federal practice runs through INM, which publishes its own marriage-authorisation form for foreigner–Mexican marriages.S10

And a second, separate conflict: an industry state-comparison table lists Jalisco as not requiring prenuptial medical examsS22S37 — which directly contradicts the official Jalisco page above.S9 We defer to the official source, because official beats industry on questions of law. But two sources disagreeing about whether a mandatory medical exam exists is not a detail you should discover on arrival.

What to do: treat every Jalisco foreigner requirement as "confirm in person or through your planner." Do not rely on any single list, including ours. Ask the Puerto Vallarta municipal Registro Civil what it requires today.

Long-established Vallarta-local industry guidance says blood tests are done locally at around US$100 per couple with results the same afternoon, and that witnesses must be 18+ and may be Mexican or foreign.S23 We could not establish when that page was last updated, so treat the cost as indicative only.

3d. Nayarit — Nuevo Vallarta, Punta Mita, Sayulita

From the Nayarit state citizen-services portal:S11

  • Witnesses: 2 (with ID copies) if you marry inside the Registro Civil office — 4 if the ceremony is outside the office. Destination weddings are almost always the second case.
  • Blood test / medical: yes — pre-nuptial medical certificate and blood analysis issued by an official health authority (S.S.N. Centro de Salud).
  • Also required: birth certificates (apostilled or legalised if foreign); certified CURP; official photo ID; divorce or death certificates if previously married; a DIF pre-marital talks certificate (pláticas prematrimoniales); and a constancia de no deudor alimentario moroso — a certificate that you are not a delinquent child-support debtor. That last one is a genuinely newer requirement that most English-language guides simply omit. Birth certificates of any children in common are also listed.
  • Official fees: MX$1,757 in office to MX$4,144 outside the office, payable by bank transfer or at a convenience store, excluding bank commissions.
  • Legal basis cited: Código Civil de Nayarit arts. 35–43, 135, 142–157, plus the state revenue law.

The Riviera Nayarit tourism board also publishes an English-language summary for couples. Note that it describes translation by an authorised translator from the Bahía de Banderas city hall and asks for documents 15 days ahead — close to but not identical with the state portal's list, which is a good illustration of why you confirm locally.S35

A number worth keeping

Government fees for a legal wedding in these states run roughly MX$1,300 to MX$7,600 — call it US$70 to US$420 at typical 2026 rates. If a wedding package's "legal fees" line is many multiples of that, you are paying for coordination rather than for government fees. That may be entirely reasonable — arranging peritos, labs and a judge in a language you don't speak is real work — but you should know which one you're buying.S5S6

5. Apostille and the perito traductor trap

This is where legal-in-Mexico timelines actually break. Not at the ceremony — three months earlier, at a records office.

What needs an apostille

  • Birth certificates — always, both spouses, if you are going the legal-in-Mexico route. BCS additionally requires the birth certificate to be recently issued;S5 industry guidance suggests most states want one issued within about six months.S22
  • Divorce decrees or a prior spouse's death certificate, if applicable — apostilled and translated.S19S11
  • Single-status / non-impediment paperwork where the office demands it (see §2).S3

Mexico is a member of the Hague Apostille Convention. An apostille is issued by the authority of the place where the document was issued — your US state's Secretary of State, or in Canada Global Affairs or the competent provincial authority.S24S15S16

Lead times and costs

United States — California as a worked example
$20 per apostille, by mail to Sacramento or same-day in person at the Sacramento or Los Angeles counters.S13 Check the current mail backlog before you rely on a mail turnaround.S14 Fees and speed vary by state — check yours. Third-party rush services run roughly US$150–200+ per document; one Florida birth-certificate service advertises $190.S26
Canada — start early, seriously
Global Affairs Canada processes authentication requests in the order received. At the time of our research it was working through requests received about 25 business days earlier, plus 5–10 business days of mailing each way, with no expedited service offered.S4 Provinces that issue their own apostilles (Ontario, Quebec, BC, Alberta, Saskatchewan) may be faster. Canadian couples should start apostilles three or more months out.

The trap: a US "certified translation" is often not enough

Translations for a Mexican Registro Civil generally must be done by a perito traductor — an expert translator authorised in Mexico. In Baja California Sur, specifically authorised by the BCS Tribunal Superior de Justicia.S5 A US certified translation, with the translator's certificate of accuracy, is not automatically acceptable.S24

And the apostille itself has to be translated too — it is part of the packet.S24 That detail catches people who translated only the certificate.

Cost anchors: US-side certified translation runs roughly US$25–55 per certificate.S25S24 Perito rates inside Mexico vary by state and language pair; your planner or the Registro Civil keeps a list of authorised peritos.

Do it in this order
  1. Order fresh certified copies of both birth certificates.
  2. Apostille them in the issuing state or province.
  3. Then have a perito translate the whole packet — often arranged in Mexico, so the perito is locally authorised.
  4. Deliver the packet to the Registro Civil by its deadline (10 days before, in BCS;S5 about a week for labs in CozumelS6).

Realistic total lead time for the apostille-plus-translation chain: 6–12 weeks if you are American (faster in same-day-counter states), and 3–4+ months for Canadians going through Global Affairs. Those two figures are our arithmetic on the official processing numbers above, not a published statistic — but the inputs are all cited.

6. Money: deposits, currency, and how vendors actually get paid

The cultural claims below are industry-sourced. They are norms, not rules. Get the currency, the amount and the refund terms into the written contract every single time.

  • Pesos or dollars? Tourist-corridor vendors in Cancún, the Riviera Maya, Cabo and Vallarta commonly quote in USD; national-market vendors quote in MXN.S27S38 Official Registro Civil fees are always in pesos. If a quote is in pesos, your dollar cost moves with the exchange rate between booking and final payment — ask whether the contract locks a rate. In Mexico a "$" sign alone is genuinely ambiguous.
  • Deposits: a retainer of 25–50% is described as standard, with the balance due somewhere between thirty days out and the day itself. A vendor asking for well under 25% may not be seriously holding your date.S27
  • Retainers are retained. Expect a deposit to be non-refundable — that is standard, and it is what wedding travel insurance exists for.S27
  • How money moves: small and independent vendors frequently prefer international wire, PayPal or Wise-type transfers, or cash on arrival for balances. Cross-border card processing is less universal than in the US and may carry surcharges. Get the payment method and who absorbs transfer fees into the contract.S27S28
  • Tips: USD and MXN are both accepted; USD is often preferred by tourist-market vendors in Cancún and the Riviera Maya, and pesos are always safe. Bring small bills. Day-of vendor tips are a cash line item US couples routinely forget to budget.S29S30
  • Exchange-rate spread is one of the recurring invisible costs — budget for a worse effective rate than the mid-market number when converting cash in-resort.S30
  • Booking window: core vendors book 10–14 months ahead for peak dates from November to April.S34
Rule of thumb

Deposits go by traceable transfer against a signed contract — never cash. Final balances follow the contract, often a transfer thirty days out or cash on the day. Tips are cash. This is our synthesis of the payment guides cited above, offered as guidance rather than as a sourced fact.

7. Picking a date around hurricane season

The two coasts have different seasons. This surprises people.

Tropical cyclone season by basin — NOAA National Hurricane Center
CoastOfficial seasonPeak
Atlantic / Caribbean
Cancún · Riviera Maya · Tulum · Cozumel		 1 June – 30 November 10 September, with most activity mid-August to mid-OctoberS12
Eastern Pacific
Los Cabos · Puerto Vallarta · Riviera Nayarit		 15 May – 30 November — starts earlier Historically clusters August–September (Hurricane Odile hit Cabo in September 2014)S12
  • Peak wedding season nationwide is November–April: warm, dry, low humidity — and resorts book out and prices peak. Couples typically plan 12–18 months ahead for these dates.S31
  • Caribbean coast best months: November–April. From June the Cancún area is hot and humid rainy season (June averages around 90°F / 77°F).S31S32
  • Los Cabos best months: October–May. The desert climate gives Cabo a longer comfortable season than the Caribbean coast; its tropical-cyclone exposure is concentrated in August and September.S31
  • A September or early-October Caribbean-coast date is the single highest-risk choice on the calendar. The statistical peak is 10 September.S12
  • If you must book June–November: buy weather-inclusive travel insurance, and confirm the resort's indoor backup venue and its hurricane/rebooking policy in writing. Resorts in these markets are experienced at this and do offer indoor backups.S31
  • Shoulder-season sweet spots with better pricing: late April–May on both coasts, and November before peak pricing lands.S31S33

8. Ten Spanish phrases for talking to vendors

Original Vow Kit content — free to use, no attribution needed. Pronunciation is approximate US-English phonetics; CAPITALS mark the stressed syllable. Politeness formulas genuinely matter in Mexican business culture: open with a greeting, use usted forms, and frame asks as questions rather than demands.

1. ¿Me puede mandar la cotización por escrito, por favor? "Can you send me the quote in writing, please?" meh PWEH-deh mahn-DAR lah koh-tee-sah-SYON por es-KREE-toh, por fah-VOR Use before any deposit. A written cotización is your paper trail.
2. ¿El precio es en pesos o en dólares? "Is the price in pesos or in dollars?" el PREH-syoh es en PEH-sohs oh en DOH-lah-rehs Ask early — a "$" sign alone is ambiguous in Mexico.
3. ¿Está incluido el IVA? "Is tax (VAT) included?" es-TAH een-kloo-EE-doh el EE-vah Mexico's 16% IVA can appear as a surprise line on an invoice.
4. ¿Cuánto es el anticipo y cuándo se paga el resto? "How much is the deposit, and when is the balance due?" KWAN-toh es el ahn-tee-SEE-poh ee KWAN-doh seh PAH-gah el RES-toh
5. ¿Me puede dar un recibo o factura, por favor? "Can you give me a receipt or official invoice, please?" meh PWEH-deh dar oon reh-SEE-boh oh fahk-TOO-rah, por fah-VOR A factura is a formal tax invoice; a recibo is a simple receipt. Ask for at least a recibo for every cash payment.
6. ¿Cuál es su política de cancelación o de mal tiempo? "What is your cancellation or bad-weather policy?" kwal es soo poh-LEE-tee-kah deh kahn-seh-lah-SYON oh deh mal TYEM-poh
7. ¿Hay algún costo adicional que deba saber? "Are there any additional costs I should know about?" eye ahl-GOON KOHS-toh ah-dee-syoh-NAHL keh DEH-bah sah-BEHR This is the one that catches transport fees, venue "outside vendor" fees, and overtime charges.
8. ¿Me puede hacer un mejor precio si pago en efectivo / si contrato dos servicios? "Can you give me a better price if I pay cash / book two services?" meh PWEH-deh ah-SEHR oon meh-HOR PREH-syoh see PAH-goh en eh-fek-TEE-voh / see kohn-TRAH-toh dohs sehr-VEE-syohs The polite Mexican way to negotiate: ask for a mejor precio, never demand a discount.
9. Está fuera de nuestro presupuesto. ¿Tiene otra opción más económica? "That's outside our budget. Do you have a more economical option?" es-TAH FWEH-rah deh NWES-troh preh-soo-PWES-toh. TYEH-neh OH-trah op-SYON mahs eh-koh-NOH-mee-kah Softer and more effective than haggling over the quoted number.
10. Perfecto, quedamos así. ¿Me lo confirma por WhatsApp o correo? "Perfect, it's agreed. Can you confirm it by WhatsApp or email?" pehr-FEK-toh, keh-DAH-mohs ah-SEE. meh loh kohn-FEER-mah por WhatsApp oh koh-REH-oh Mexican vendors run on WhatsApp — it is the default business channel.S34 Always convert a verbal agreement into a written message.

Bonus etiquette: open every message with "Hola, buenas tardes" (OH-lah BWEH-nahs TAR-dehs) and close with "¡Muchas gracias, saludos!" (MOO-chahs GRAH-syahs sah-LOO-dohs). Warmth is currency.

9. What we could not verify, and where sources conflict

This section exists because it is the most useful thing on the page. Any guide can list requirements. Very few will tell you which of their own claims are shaky.

  1. Jalisco is the weakest state section here. The official state page still references "FM3" and a "Permiso de Gobernación," suggesting it has not been updated for the post-2011 migration framework — while an industry table claims Jalisco dropped prenuptial exams, contradicting that same official page. Resolution: defer to whatever the Puerto Vallarta municipal Registro Civil says today.S9S22
  2. The Quintana Roo "3–4 business days in-country" figure is industry guidance. No official QR statute stating an exact day count was found. Treat it as a planning buffer.S18
  3. CURP for foreigners is unresolved in the published sources. Both the BCS and Nayarit requirement lists include CURP without explaining the foreigner case. Offices handle it in practice; we cannot tell you how from a document, so ask.S5S11
  4. The ~US$100/couple Vallarta blood-test figure comes from a long-standing local page whose date we could not establish. Indicative range only.S23
  5. Two sources we deliberately did not build claims on. The Mexican consular (SRE/Consulmex) foreigner-marriage page was bot-walled when we tried to read it in full, so the only version we had was a search snippet: consulmex.sre.gob.mx. And the US Embassy Mexico marriage page returned a technical error at research time: mx.usembassy.gov/marriage — it responds again as of 8 September 2026, but since we never read it during research, no claim on this page rests on it. Both are worth your time to read directly.
  6. All peso fee figures are 2026 published schedules and change with each state's annual revenue law (Ley de Ingresos). UMA-indexed fees — Playa del Carmen's, for example — reset every February when the UMA is republished.
  7. The 6–12 week and 3–4 month apostille lead times are our arithmetic on the official processing figures in §5, not published statistics. The inputs are cited; the sum is our judgement.
