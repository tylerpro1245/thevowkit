About

Who's behind this, and what we haven't got yet

Most "about" pages are an exercise in sounding established. This one is going to tell you exactly what is here and exactly what isn't, because the second half is the part you can't get anywhere else.

The short version

I'm Tyler Nielson. I work in destination weddings in Los Cabos, Baja California Sur, Mexico, and I'm natively bilingual in English and Spanish. I write and build these kits myself.

The Vow Kit exists because of one recurring conversation. A couple arrives with a spreadsheet from a US wedding blog and a plan that assumes Mexico works like Nevada. Then somebody mentions the blood test — the one that has to be done here, that is only valid fifteen days, that nobody's timeline accounted for. Or the birth certificate that was translated by a perfectly good certified translator in Ohio, which the Registro Civil will not accept, because it wants a perito authorised by the state's own high court.

None of that is obscure. It is written down, in Spanish, on municipal government pages that don't rank in English search results. The gap isn't knowledge. It's that nobody has bothered to put it in front of the person who needs it, in their language, with the source attached.

What I'm claiming, precisely

True, and the reason to listen
  • I work in destination weddings in Los Cabos, BCS.
  • I am natively bilingual EN/ES — the Spanish in these files is written, not translated by machine and cleaned up.
  • I build every file in these kits myself, and I answer every message myself.
  • Every legal, fee and date-sensitive claim we publish carries its source and the date it was checked. Where sources conflict, the conflict is printed rather than resolved by preference.
Not true, and I'm not going to imply it
  • I am not a lawyer, a notary, or a Mexican civil-registry official. Nothing here is legal advice, and I will always send you to the Registro Civil for your specific municipality.
  • I am not a licensed wedding planner selling planning services. These are documents, not coordination.
  • This shop is brand new. It opened in September 2026. Two listings. Zero reviews. No sales history to point at. No "trusted by 3,000 couples."
  • I have no testimonials — because I have no customers yet to quote, and inventing them is the one thing that would make everything else on this site worthless.

The obvious objection

"Why would I buy a $24 file from someone with no reviews?"

That is the right question, and I don't have a clever answer to it. I have three plain ones.

  1. Read the free thing first. The Mexico reference is the same work, the same standard, given away in full. Thirty-eight sources, every claim dated, a whole section listing what I could not verify. If that page is good, the paid material is by the same hand. If it isn't, you've lost twenty minutes and nothing else.
  2. Every review-based shop had a first week. Somebody buys before there are reviews, or there are never reviews. What I can offer in place of social proof is unusual specificity: I'll tell you the page count, what's in every file, what format it opens in, and what it does not include — before you pay.
  3. Checkout runs through Etsy, not through me. Your payment and your buyer protection sit with a platform you already have an account with. I don't touch your card details, and Etsy's refund process doesn't depend on my goodwill.

How the files are actually made

Worth saying plainly, because a lot of digital wedding products are a stock template with a new colour on it.

  • Research first, prose second. Every factual claim is collected with its source URL and a source tag — Official for a government source, Industry for an established trade source — before anything is written. Anything that couldn't be sourced either gets flagged as judgement or gets cut.
  • The Spanish is liturgical and legal Spanish, not translated English. In the ceremony files that means Latin-American ustedes, the fixed ritual forms ("Sí, quiero." / "Yo, N., te recibo a ti, N., …"), and arras, lazo and padrinos placed where the rite actually puts them — arras after the rings, lazo on after the Our Father and off when the Nuptial Blessing ends.
  • Nothing ships until it's been looked at as a buyer receives it. Every page gets rendered and inspected, and the sales copy gets diffed line-by-line against what's actually in the download. A promise the file doesn't keep outranks every cosmetic defect.
  • AI assistance is used, and disclosed. Research gathering, drafting and file generation use AI tools. The Spanish is written and checked by a native bilingual speaker. The editorial calls — what to flag, what to cut, what to admit we don't know — are mine. You're spending real money on decisions that cost real money to get wrong, so you should know how the information reached you.

What I actually want from this

I'd like the Mexico page to be the best free resource on the topic on the internet. Not as a marketing metaphor — literally the page a stranger would send to a friend who's just got engaged and picked Tulum.

That is also, transparently, the business model: a page that good earns enough trust that some readers buy a kit. I'd rather tell you the mechanism than pretend the free thing has no commercial purpose. It does. It's still free, still complete, and still there whether or not you buy anything.

Corrections

If you find something on this site that is wrong, out of date, or that your Registro Civil office contradicted in person — tell me. Corrections get made, the check-date moves, and I'll credit you if you want the credit. People who have actually stood at the counter know things no published page does.
