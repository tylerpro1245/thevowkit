Who writes this

The Vow Kit is one person, not a company with a team page. Here is the whole of it, including the parts that are not flattering.

Tyler Nielson

I work in destination weddings in Los Cabos, Baja California Sur, Mexico. I am natively bilingual in English and Spanish, which is the entire reason this site exists: the useful sources on getting married in Mexico are Mexican government pages written in Spanish, and most English-language wedding advice is a summary of a summary of one of them.

That is the whole of the credential. I am not a lawyer, not a notario, not a licensed travel agent, and not a Registro Civil official. I do not have privileged access to anything. What I have is the ability to read the BCS Registro Civil's own page in the language it was written in, and enough working proximity to know which of its lines cause problems in practice.

The shop is brand new. It has no reviews and no sales history.

Both Etsy listings are new. There are no customer reviews, no ratings, no “trusted by 1,200 couples,” no testimonials, and no years-in-business number — because all of those would be fabrications, and I would rather lose the sale.

You will find no countdown timers, no “only 3 left,” and no invented scarcity anywhere on this site either. The products are digital files. There is no such thing as running out of them.

Why a new shop is not a reason to doubt the facts

Reviews are evidence about a seller: did the file arrive, was the reply fast, was the description honest. That is a real and useful signal, and I do not have it yet. Ignoring that would be dishonest.

But reviews are not evidence about a fact. Whether Baja California Sur requires documents ten days before the appointment does not become more or less true because a stranger liked their download. That claim is settled by opening the BCS Registro Civil page and reading it — which is exactly why every claim on the requirements reference carries its source link and check date instead of asking you to trust me.

The correct way to read this site is to not trust it. Click the citations. They go to state governments, gob.mx, NOAA, the US State Department, and the Government of Canada. If a link ever contradicts what I have written on the page, the link is right and I want to know: hello@thevowkit.com.

How the research was done, including the AI part

The underlying fact sheet behind this site holds 74 individually cited claims, each tagged for whether it came from a government source or an industry one, with explicit flags where official sources contradict each other.

AI assistance was used to gather and organise those sources and to draft this site's text. Every legal requirement was then checked against the primary source — the actual government page — and the tags, conflict flags and “we could not verify this” notes exist precisely because that check found things worth flagging. I am telling you this because you would reasonably want to know, and because it makes the citation links more important, not less.

Two of those admissions, as examples: the SRE consular page on foreigners marrying in Mexico was blocked by a bot wall when we tried to read it, so nothing on this site is built on it. The US Embassy Mexico marriage page returned an error, so we cited travel.state.gov instead. Both are written down in the conflicts and gaps section.

What I will not do

  • Invent reviews, testimonials, customer counts, or a founding year.
  • Manufacture urgency for a digital download.
  • State a legal requirement without linking the source and dating the check.
  • Resolve a conflict between two sources by quietly picking the one that reads better.
  • Claim to be a lawyer, or let the site imply it. This is not legal advice, and the office that marries you is the only authority that counts.

Corrections

Requirements change every year — state revenue laws are rewritten annually and UMA-indexed fees reset each February. If you have just been through this process and an office told you something different from what is on this site, that is more current than my citation, and I would rather publish your correction than defend a stale page.
