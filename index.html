What Mexico actually requires to marry you

Mexico has thirty-two marriage laws, not one. We wrote down what four of the states couples actually get married in require — Quintana Roo, Baja California Sur, Jalisco, Nayarit — with the government page each requirement came from and the date we checked it.

The three things it will tell you that a resort brochure won't

1. Nine couples in ten should not do the legal part in Mexico

The paperwork route adds apostilled and translated documents, a blood test taken in Mexico, four witnesses, several business days in-country, and roughly US$700–2,000+ in legal-only costs in the Cancún area. The State Department itself describes the alternative — marry at your local courthouse, then have exactly the ceremony you want in Mexico — as the way to avoid “the complexity of obtaining a foreign marriage certificate.”

Official travel.state.gov — Marriage abroad; cost range Industry blog.destinationweddings.com — checked 7 Sep 2026

2. The blood test expires before your paperwork clears

The pre-marital medical certificate has to be issued in Mexico, and it is valid about 15 days. Meanwhile Baja California Sur wants your complete document packet submitted 10 days before the appointment, and Playa del Carmen lists a 20-business-day resolution for the trámite. Those two clocks cannot run at the same time — which is the scheduling failure that wrecks otherwise well-planned legal weddings.

Official cozumel.gob.mx; registrocivil.bcs.gob.mx; playadelcarmen.gob.mx — checked 7 Sep 2026. How to sequence around it →

3. Puerto Vallarta and its hotel strip are in different states

Puerto Vallarta is in Jalisco. Nuevo Vallarta, Punta Mita and Sayulita — twenty minutes north — are in Nayarit. Different witness counts, different fees, different extra certificates. Nayarit, for instance, requires a constancia de no deudor alimentario moroso, a certificate that you are not behind on child support, which almost no English-language checklist mentions.

Official tramites.nayarit.gob.mx; sgg.jalisco.gob.mx — checked 7 Sep 2026

How this reference is built

  • Every claim carries its source and the date it was checked. Nothing is asserted from memory or from another blog's summary of a government page.
  • Sources are labelled. Official means a government page — a state Registro Civil, gob.mx, the US State Department, the Government of Canada. Industry means an established wedding-trade source: fine for costs and norms, weaker for law, and never used to override an official page.
  • Conflicts are shown, not smoothed. Jalisco's official page contradicts an industry comparison table and cites an immigration form abolished years ago. Cozumel says the blood test lasts 15 business days; BCS says 15 days without specifying. We print the disagreement and tell you which question to ask the office.
  • Gaps are admitted. The widely repeated “3–4 days in-country” rule for Quintana Roo has no official statute behind it that we could find, so we label it planning guidance rather than law.

The limit of any page on the internet, including this one

Mexican marriage law is state law, and the fees are municipal. Requirements change annually. The only authority that counts is the Registro Civil office that will actually marry you. Use this page to know what to ask and what a wrong answer looks like — then confirm with that office before you book flights or pay deposits.

Two more things worth knowing before you pick a date

Hurricane season is not the same on both coasts

The Atlantic and Caribbean basin — Cancún, Riviera Maya, Tulum, Cozumel — runs 1 June to 30 November, and the statistical peak is 10 September. The Eastern Pacific basin, covering Los Cabos, Puerto Vallarta and Riviera Nayarit, opens two weeks earlier, on 15 May. A mid-September Caribbean-coast date is the highest-risk choice on the calendar.

Official NOAA National Hurricane Center — climatology — checked 7 Sep 2026

A deposit under 25% is a warning sign, not a bargain

A retainer of 25–50% is standard for Mexican wedding vendors, with the balance due somewhere between thirty days out and the day itself — and the retainer is genuinely retained, not a refundable down payment. That is what wedding travel insurance is for.
