What I can actually help with

  • "Is my venue in Jalisco or Nayarit?" — a genuinely common and genuinely expensive confusion. Puerto Vallarta is Jalisco; Nuevo Vallarta, Punta Mita and Sayulita are Nayarit, twenty minutes up the road and under a different set of rules.
  • "We're Canadian and the wedding is in five months — is the apostille timeline survivable?" I'll tell you honestly, including when the answer is "do the legal part at home instead."
  • "Does the program kit cover a ceremony outside Mass?" Yes, and I'll tell you which of the two booklets you want before you buy rather than after.
  • "My priest wants X wording." Send me what he said. Parish customs vary and I'd rather you know in advance whether the file matches.
  • "You've got something wrong on the free guide." Please. Especially if you've stood at the counter and been told something different — that's information no published page has.
  • Preguntas en español. Perfectamente bien. Escríbeme en español si prefieres.

What I can't help with

Boundaries, stated up front
  • I can't give you legal advice. I'm not a lawyer or a civil-registry official. I can tell you what an office's published page says and what the reliable question to ask is — I can't tell you what your marriage will be worth in a court.
  • I can't confirm what a specific office will accept today. Only that office can. Anyone who tells you otherwise about a Mexican municipal requirement is guessing with confidence.
  • I'm not a wedding planner and I'm not selling coordination. If what you need is somebody on the ground running your day, you need a planner, not a document.
  • I can't promise a response time. This is one person, not a support desk. I answer everything, usually within a day or two.

What makes a question easy to answer well

None of this is required — send whatever you've got. But if you include these, the first reply will be useful instead of a list of clarifying questions:

  • The town, not just the state. "Playa del Carmen" and "Cozumel" are both Quintana Roo and have different fees and different paperwork.
  • Your date, even approximate — it decides whether the timeline advice is "you're fine" or "start the apostille this week."
  • Legal in Mexico, or symbolic? If you don't know yet, say that — it's the most useful thing to work out first, and §1 of the free guide is written to settle it in about five minutes.
  • Nationality of each of you. US and Canadian document routes genuinely differ, and Canada's is much slower.
  • Whether either of you has been married before. It adds an apostilled, translated document to the pile.
Before you write

There's a decent chance the answer is already on the free page, in more detail than a message would carry: state-by-state requirements, apostille and translation lead times, hurricane timing by coast, and an honest list of what I could not verify. Ask anyway if you'd rather hear it from a person.
